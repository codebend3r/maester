# Using raven's device app

How the app reaches raven, plays a title and keeps its settings. Part of [raven's device app](../README.md).

## Reaching raven

The app talks to raven at its public origin, `https://raven-player.synology.me:8443` (core's `DEFAULT_SERVER_ORIGIN`), as the desktop does, and asks for the account's email and password. It never asks for the PIN, and a locked library stays unavailable. The account session and the server's cookies stay in the device's keychain or keystore.

A release build reaches that HTTPS address only. A development build may use plain HTTP for Metro and for the end to end fixture: `APP_VARIANT=development` sets `NSAllowsLocalNetworking` on Apple and `usesCleartextTraffic` on Android. The fixture build also sets `EXPO_PUBLIC_RAVEN_BYPASS_ORIGIN=http://127.0.0.1:8490`, which skips account sign-in on that accountless server and starts at its PIN.

Under an account every video plays by the account ticket the app asks the server for as it opens the video, from the fastest media origin or the server's own address; the player sends no cookie and no token. A direct file plays on the pass its ticket earns by opening it, and a remux or a transcode on its stream's own pass, which the server ties to that file or that stream, so a film longer than the ticket's five minutes plays to its end. When the server lets go of a video mid-play (a pass or a stream it ended or forgot after its twelve hours, a restart, or too many at once), the app opens it again from the same spot and says nothing. If the server will not hand out the ticket, the player says why in raven player's words: "Sign in to X again." or "Your access to X was removed."

To install a build on your own iPhone, iPad, Apple TV or Android TV, follow the [raven README](../../README.md#raven-app-iphone-ipad-apple-tv-android-tv).

## Playing

On an iPhone or iPad a title plays under raven's own controls: Back, the title and the audio and subtitle pickers along the top, play and pause between ten-second skips, and the time over a line to drag. A tap hides or shows them, and they fade by themselves once the video has played untouched for the linger the player preferences keep (five seconds unless changed). Picture in Picture starts from its button or when the app leaves the screen mid-play, AirPlay is in Control Center, and the screen stays on while the video plays.

On Apple TV a title plays under raven's own controls too, the ones Android TV has (below), driven by the Siri Remote: a press plays or pauses, left and right on the line skip ten seconds, and the audio, subtitle and quality pickers open raven's own lists. The system's player, its info panel and its Subtitles and Audio panels are gone. raven draws the subtitles itself, as on an iPhone, in the account's subtitle look, so a file with subtitles (a sidecar or a text track inside it) plays as it is; a picture subtitle (Blu-ray PGS, VobSub) is burned into the picture by the server. Menu leaves the player and saves the spot.

On Android TV a title plays under raven's own controls, driven by the d-pad: the title along the top, and the time over a line, then Play or Pause, the audio and subtitle pickers and Back along the bottom. The line has the focus as the controls appear, and left and right on it skip ten seconds; down moves to the buttons. The controls fade once the video has played untouched for the player preferences' linger, and any press brings them back; while they are hidden, left and right still skip, and the remote's play, rewind and fast forward keys work wherever the focus is. The TV's own player draws the subtitles, on its own clock, so a line shows as it is said however busy the app is, and a file with subtitles still plays as it is. They wear the account's subtitle look, Settings' Subtitles: Young Serif in the size and colour picked, the same for every video on every device. A file whose sound the TV can neither play nor pass on (DTS or TrueHD on many TVs) counts as one that failed to start, so it plays through the remux, whose sound the server makes Dolby Digital Plus. Back leaves the player and saves the spot.

Everywhere else on Apple TV, Menu goes back a screen, and on Home it leaves raven for the TV's home screen, as tvOS's own apps do. Going back lands on the card you opened, not on the first one, and moving down or up into a row of cards lands on one of its cards: its first the first time, then the one you last left there. A library shelf's See all sits above its row, beside the shelf's title, and is reached as anything else on the screen is, by moving towards it.

A file the device plays as it is (on Apple, an MP4 or MOV of H.264 or HEVC with audio it decodes; on Android TV, also MKV and WebM, AV1 and VP9, and Opus, Vorbis and MP3) plays straight from the server; anything else plays through the server's HLS remux, or its transcode when the video itself must change. When one way fails to start, or has not started within 15 seconds, the next is tried from the same spot. Play and Resume start where the item's page says, the spot is saved every ten seconds, on a pause and on leaving, and the play counts in the library's history as soon as it starts.

## Settings

Settings opens from the gear beside Search at the top of Home on an iPhone or iPad, and from the Settings button beside Search on a TV. Everything in it stays on the device; it changes nothing on any server.

- **Account.** Who is signed in, with Sign out, which goes back to sign-in.
- **Theme.** One of the web's seven themes, from the system's sheet under the thumb or a list on a TV. Every screen takes it up at once, with no restart, and it is the theme raven opens in next time. Patina and Mint are light, and wear the logo drawn for a light ground; on them the system's own sheets, alerts and keyboards are light too. The player looks the same in every theme, dark over the picture as on the web: its buttons, track chips and the lists and sheets they open are dark glass, and only its played line takes the theme's colour. raven opens on its splash in the default theme's dark and holds it until the theme picked is ready, so it never flashes white or the wrong theme.
- **Playback.** These only ever narrow what the server allows. Prefer direct play (on unless turned off) tries a file the device may refuse as it is first, such as HEVC in MP4 on Apple or DTS on Android TV; off, such a file starts at the server's remux. Allow transcoding (on) lets the server re-encode what the device cannot play; off, the server only remuxes, and a file that needs a transcode says so. Away from home caps a transcode played over the internet to save data: at 1080p (the default) it asks the server's lowest 1080p size, 8 Mb/s, at 720p its lowest 720p size, 2 Mb/s, and at 4K it asks nothing, leaving the server's own settings.
